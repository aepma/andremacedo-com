him the evidence he never had — the two independent craft critics' persisted

and what the rest of TELOS has been doing — and demands a verdict. A missing or
malformed verdict rejects the generation. Four consecutive deferrals against a flat
series, or 45 days of age, clears the epoch mechanically and says so out loud.

### C. Plateau detector — RUNS CORRECTLY, but the current series is NOT flat

Against the seeded history, scoped to the live epoch's window:

```
$ python3 scripts/epoch_review.py plateau --history state/craft-history.jsonl --since 2026-07-20
{
  "window": 4, "threshold": 0.5, "since": "2026-07-20",
  "scored_available": 4, "excluded_unobtainable": 2,
  "series": [{"gen": 234, "min_overall": 7.6}, {"gen": 235, "min_overall": 7.6},
             {"gen": 236, "min_overall": 7.5}, {"gen": 237, "min_overall": 7.0}],
  "spread": 0.6, "flat": false, "verdict": "NOT_FLAT"
}
exit=0
```

**Computed spread 0.6, verdict NOT_FLAT.** The brief expected a plateau. It is not
one, and the reason matters: gen 237 did not repeat the band, it dropped 0.5 below
it. Under the specified rule — spread between highest and lowest `min_overall` across
the last four scored entries, flat when under 0.5 — a decline reads as movement. The
same holds if you average the two critics instead of taking the lower (spread 0.65).
I implemented the rule as specified rather than quietly widening it to "flat or
declining"; see Unanticipated findings below, because this is a real hole in the
backstop's first arm and it is your call, not mine.

Unobtainable exclusion, on a synthetic series of four scored entries with two
unobtainable entries interleaved between them:

```

exit=0
$ python3 scripts/epoch_review.py plateau --history synth-clean.jsonl   # same file, unobtainable lines deleted
```

## Things in the live files this brief did not anticipate

1. **The current series is not flat; it is falling.** Assertion C above. The
   backstop's first arm (4 deepens + spread under 0.5) cannot fire on a decline,
   which is the failure mode the epoch actually just had. Only the 45-day arm would
   have caught epoch 9. Widening "flat" to "flat or declining" is a one-line change
   and a judgement call about how much authority the machine should have; I left the
   rule exactly as specified.
2. **The rejection point is record-generation.py, not the runner's coherence-teeth
   block.** The brief pointed at "the way a missing fitness axis is already treated",
   but no missing-axis check exists — the coherence teeth only enforce a numeric
   coherence floor, and they run *after* record-generation.py has already consumed
   and acted on the verdict. Validating at the top of record-generation.py rejects
   before any state is mutated and reuses the runner's existing fail-closed path for
   a failed record step (revert index.html, no deploy, failure counter tripped). No
   parallel mechanism was invented.
3. **The 07-05 to 07-19 weekly commits are absent from this repository's history**
   ("re-establish git history after worktree parent removal", 2026-07-30). The
   changelog carried the mapping instead.
4. **`genome.epoch` and `genome.epoch_started` are stale right now** ("resonance",
   2026-08-02) because the hand-clearing did not touch them. The burial path
   deliberately mirrors that, leaving both for the next obsession birth to set. The
   prompt's genome header consequently reads `epoch 10 "resonance"` during a
   clearing. Worth a decision, not fixed here.
5. **The epitaph provenance markers cover epochs 3, 4, 5 and 8.** Each of those
   epitaphs announces the *successor's* obsession as a new direction (epoch 3 ends
   "Now: TYPOGRAPHY AS ARCHITECTURE…", epoch 8 is verbatim the rationale for
   resonance). Epochs 1, 2, 6 and 7 read as genuine eulogies of their own epoch and
   correspond to hand-run resets, so they are unmarked; epoch 9, written today, is
   unmarked. Nothing was rewritten or invented.
6. **`scripts/__pycache__` is tracked in git.** `python3 -m py_compile` rewrote some
   of those `.pyc` files; they were restored to HEAD and are not in the commit.
